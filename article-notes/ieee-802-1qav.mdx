## Where the delay of a frame comes from

Zero congestion loss rests on computing buffer needs for the **worst case**. YSH19, after Finn (2018), splits the delay of a packet through one relay into five parts:

<SourceFigure
  id="yang2019-timing-model"
  caption="Figure 1. The TSN timing model: output delay, link delay, preemption delay, processing delay and queuing delay."
/>

1. **Output delay**: from selection for transmission to the first bit on the wire.
2. **Link delay**: propagation on the cable.
3. **Preemption delay**: waiting for an interrupted or non-interruptible frame (see [preemption](./ieee-802-1qbu-802-3br.mdx)).
4. **Processing delay**: from reception to entry into the output queue.
5. **Queuing delay**: waiting in the output queue. Only this part depends on the other traffic and the shaper, and it is the one that the shaper makes analysable.

Variations in the first four are absorbed by buffers; the fifth is bounded by analysing the queuing algorithm, which is what [network calculus](/blog/network-calculus-for-tsn) does.

## How the shaper works

[IEEE 802.1Qav](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/802.1Qav/4401/), from the AVB suite, gives real-time guarantees **without** synchronised clocks (LS19 §III-B). Each shaped queue has a **credit**:

<SourceFigure
  id="yang2019-cbs-flowchart"
  caption="Figure 2. The CBS decision for one queue. A frame may start only when frames are queued, the credit is non-negative and the port is free."
/>

| Situation | Credit |
| --- | --- |
| Frames waiting, not transmitting (port busy or credit negative) | Increases at the **idleSlope** |
| Transmitting a frame of this queue | Decreases at the **sendSlope** = idleSlope − port rate |
| Queue empty and credit positive | Reset to zero |
| Queue empty and credit negative | Increases back towards zero |

:::caution Correction to the seminar paper
YSH19 writes that credit *increases when a frame is added to the queue and decreases when a frame is sent*. This is a simplification: credit grows with **time spent waiting** at the idle slope, not with the number of frames enqueued, and it decreases with **time spent sending**. Its own figure (Figure 2) shows the correct rule. See the [CBS post](/blog/credit-based-shaper) for the exact rules.
:::

The parameters that bound the per-bridge latency are the **idle slope** (the reserved bandwidth), the **maximum frame size** of the class and of interfering lower classes, and the **port rate** (YSH19 lists them as maximum frame size, maximum reference size and maximum port transmission rate).

## CBS with frame preemption

802.1Qbu specifies how CBS behaves when preemption is on (LS19 §IV-C):

- Time during which a preemptable frame of a CBS queue is held or interrupted by express traffic **does not consume credit**: the shaper's *transmit* flag stays false until the frame starts or resumes.
- The extra overhead of preemption (fragment headers) is **not** subtracted from the bandwidth reserved for the shaper.

Without scheduled windows, a CBS on express traffic also limits how long preemptable frames are delayed, which keeps their transit time within the bridge bound.
