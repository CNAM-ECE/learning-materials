A **profile** selects, among the many options of the TSN standards, the features, settings and protocols that one application domain needs, so that devices from different vendors interoperate.

## IEEE 802.1CM: fronthaul

- [IEEE 802.1CM](https://1.ieee802.org/tsn/802-1cm-2018/) (2018) is the TSN profile for **mobile fronthaul**: the link between radio equipment and its controller, carrying CPRI or eCPRI over bridged Ethernet (YSH19 §6.1).
- Fronthaul networks are large (kilometre links) and fast (10 Gbit/s). Simulations show Qbv and Qbu help, and a **playout buffer** at the receiver improves further (Simon et al., in LS19 §V).
- TSN also helps **network slicing** in 5G (no interference between users) and the aggregation of flows in bearer networks. Integration of 5G and TSN is a research topic of its own.

The fronthaul figure of YSH19 is the same IEEE 802.1 slide as Figure 5 of the [802.1CM post](/blog/ieee-802-1cm-fronthaul-profile).

## IEC/IEEE 60802: industrial automation

**Why.** Industry 4.0 brings IT (cloud, services) and OT (control with real-time and safety needs) onto one network. Cheap high-speed Ethernet makes critical and non-critical flows share links; TSN gives the guarantees, but vendors and users need a common selection of features. A consortium of automation suppliers announced TSN with **OPC UA** as their future standard ("the fieldbus war is over").

**What it selects (as planned in 2019).** IEEE 802.3, 802.1Q, 802.1AB (LLDP), 802.1AS-Rev, and optionally 802.1CB and 802.1X, plus coexistence with the IEC 61784-2 communication profile families. Both Layer 2 and the protocols that configure it are covered.

**Traffic types** from the use-case document: isochronous cyclic real-time, cyclic real-time, network control, audio/video, alarms and events, configuration and diagnostics, internal pass-through, best effort, and **brownfield** traffic from non-TSN devices.

**TSN domains.** A TSN domain is a set of commonly managed devices, ports and links: a machine, a production cell, a line. A device can belong to several domains, and domains are joined by bridges (Layer 2), routers (Layer 3) or gateways (Layer 7).

**Two use cases with requirements:**

| Use case | Requirement | TSN mechanisms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sequence of events (plant-wide timestamped events) | Universal time, deviation from the grandmaster between 1 µs and 100 µs, redundancy | [802.1AS-Rev](./ieee-802-1as.mdx) |
| Machine to machine / controller to controller | Machine-internal traffic isolated from M2M traffic; one-to-one and one-to-many; schedules interleaved with machine cycles | [Qbu](./ieee-802-1qbu-802-3br.mdx), [Qbv](./ieee-802-1qbv.mdx), [Qci](./ieee-802-1qci.mdx), 802.3br, fixed priority, priority regeneration, queue-based allocation, VLANs per domain |

**Fog computing.** Extending switches with compute and storage helps IT/OT convergence, but needs **run-time reconfiguration**; a configuration agent based on 802.1Qcc and OPC UA, with heuristic rescheduling, was shown feasible.

XZZ23 recommends taking evaluation scenarios from such profiles so that scheduling results are comparable. The profile was later published as IEC/IEEE 60802; see the [60802 post](/blog/iec-ieee-60802-industrial-profile).

## Automotive in-vehicle networks

Cars use CAN, LIN, MOST and FlexRay; Ethernet arrived late but is spreading fast, and TSN lets one network replace several. LS19 describes three uses:

| Use | What TSN brings |
| --- | --- |
| **Backbone between domain controllers** (body, energy, connectivity, infotainment, chassis, ADAS) | Qbv for time-critical messages; CB for medium-criticality redundancy (high-criticality redundancy stays in the application, see [802.1CB](./ieee-802-1cb.mdx)); Qci against babbling ECUs; AS for synchronised tasks and timestamps |
| **Sensor bus** (cameras, radar, lidar, ultrasound) | Qbv, CB and Qci, plus centralised configuration (Qcc) so that sensors can be reassigned to ECUs; gateways that bundle sensor outputs into frames |
| **Inside an ECU** | Deterministic Ethernet between chips on one board, or between boards |

Why automotive lags industry: Ethernet in cars is recent. Signs of adoption: car makers in the TSN task group, automotive TSN conferences, automotive-grade products. The profile became [IEEE 802.1DG](/blog/ieee-802-1dg-automotive-profile).

## Other domains (LS19 §V)

- **Trains**: future in-train networks based on TSN, possibly with SAE AS6802 and ARINC 664.
- **Aircraft**: a cabin backbone bus using Qbv, AS and CB.
- **Agricultural vehicles**: TSN as a candidate for a high-speed ISOBUS.
- **Professional audio/video and industrial M2M**, also cited as DetNet use cases (YSH19 §6.2).
