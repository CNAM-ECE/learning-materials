A flow in a TSN network crosses several bridges. There are two ways to bound its end-to-end delay: add the per-hop bounds, or first compose the hops into a single equivalent server. The second is never worse and often much better. This post explains why.

## 1. Concatenation theorem

If a flow crosses system $S_1$ offering service curve $\beta_1$ and then system $S_2$ offering $\beta_2$, the concatenation offers the service curve

$$
\beta_{1,2} = \beta_1 \otimes \beta_2 .
$$

The proof is a one-line consequence of associativity: $R_2^* \ge R_1^* \otimes \beta_2 \ge (R \otimes \beta_1) \otimes \beta_2 = R \otimes (\beta_1 \otimes \beta_2)$. The result extends to any number of hops by induction.

For rate-latency servers, [post 2](/blog/min-plus-algebra) gives the closed form

$$
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n} \beta_{R_i, T_i} = \beta_{\min_i R_i,\; \sum_i T_i}.
$$

The path behaves like a single server whose rate is the bottleneck rate and whose latency is the sum of the latencies.

## 2. Two ways to bound a two-hop path

Consider a token-bucket flow $\gamma_{r,b}$ crossing two rate-latency servers $\beta_{R_1,T_1}$ and $\beta_{R_2,T_2}$, with $r \le \min(R_1, R_2)$.

**Additive (hop-by-hop) analysis.** At hop 1 the delay is $T_1 + b/R_1$ and the output burst is $b + rT_1$. At hop 2 the delay is $T_2 + (b + rT_1)/R_2$. Summing,

$$
D_{\text{add}} = T_1 + T_2 + \frac{b}{R_1} + \frac{b + r T_1}{R_2}.
$$

**Concatenated analysis.** With $R = \min(R_1, R_2)$,

$$
D_{\text{conc}} = T_1 + T_2 + \frac{b}{R}.
$$

The additive bound pays the burst $b$ at *every* hop, and additionally pays the burst *increase* created by upstream latencies. The concatenated bound pays the burst once, at the bottleneck. This is the **pay bursts only once** (PBOO) principle.

## 3. Numerical example

Take two identical hops with $R = 100$ Mbit/s and $T = 20\ \mu s$, and the flow $b = 12\,000$ bits, $r = 12$ Mbit/s from the previous posts.

| Method | Formula | Bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Additive | $(20 + 120) + (20 + 122.4)$ | $282.4\ \mu s$ |
| Concatenated | $40 + 120$ | $160\ \mu s$ |

The concatenated bound is about 43 % smaller. The gap grows linearly with the number of hops: with $n$ identical hops, the additive bound grows like $n\,b/R$ whereas the concatenated bound keeps a single $b/R$ term.

## 4. Why this matters for TSN

PBOO is the reason per-flow, path-based analyses can be much tighter than per-node analyses. It is not free, however.

- It requires a *per-flow* service curve at each hop. When flows share a queue, one must first derive the service left to the flow of interest, the **residual service curve**, which is the subject of the [next post](/blog/aggregate-multiplexing-residual-service).
- For aggregate scheduling, a related principle, **pay multiplexing only once** (PMOO), avoids paying the interference of a cross flow at every hop it shares with the flow of interest. PMOO is established for blind multiplexing; FIFO multiplexing requires different techniques.

The trade-off between per-node analyses (simple, incremental, fast) and path-based analyses (tighter, more expensive) recurs in the discussion of online admission control in [post 9](/blog/dynamic-reconfiguration-of-tsn).
