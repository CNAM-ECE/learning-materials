Strict priority and gates decide which frame starts next. Neither can stop a frame that has already started. A 1522-byte frame occupies a 1 Gbit/s link for about 12 µs, and an urgent frame that arrives just after it must wait that long. Frame preemption removes most of that wait.

## 1. Definition

**Frame preemption** lets an *express* frame suspend the transmission of a *preemptable* frame. The preemptable frame resumes from where it stopped once the express traffic has been sent. Two standards share the work [2][3]:

- **[IEEE 802.3br](https://www.ieee802.org/3/br/)**, *Interspersing Express Traffic*, changes [Ethernet](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/802.3/10422/). It splits the MAC into an express MAC and a preemptable MAC and adds a **MAC Merge sublayer** between them and the physical layer.
- **[IEEE 802.1Qbu](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/802.1Qbu/5464/)**, now part of [802.1Q](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/802.1Q/10323/), changes the bridge. It assigns each priority a **frame preemption status**, express or preemptable, and coordinates preemption with scheduled traffic.

## 2. Architecture

<MacMergeFigure caption="Figure 2. The MAC split of 802.3br. The bridge port hands each frame to the express or the preemptable MAC according to its priority. The MAC Merge sublayer interleaves them on one link." />

The interruption is **local to one link**. The receiving MAC Merge sublayer reassembles the fragments before the frame goes up to the next bridge's relay [3]. Preemption is therefore not [IP](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc791) fragmentation, and the frame is never seen in pieces by any higher layer. The rules that make this work:

- each fragment, including its check sequence, is at least **64 bytes** [3];
- an express frame is never preempted;
- a preemptable frame may be interrupted more than once;
- both ends of the link must support preemption, and 802.3br verifies this before the feature is used.

## 3. What it changes on the wire

<PreemptionFigure caption="Figure 3. Without preemption, the express frame waits for the whole preemptable frame. With preemption, the preemptable frame is cut, the express frame goes out, and the remainder follows." />

Because of the 64-byte rule, a preemptable frame shorter than 128 bytes cannot be split at all, and the tail of a longer frame cannot be cut once less than 64 bytes remain. An express frame can therefore still wait for a short piece of preemptable traffic, of the order of a hundred bytes. At 1 Gbit/s that is about 1 µs instead of about 12 µs.

## 4. Preemption and the guard band

<CommonsFigure
  id="frame-preemption"
  caption="Figure 4. Preemption combined with an 802.1Qbv schedule. The best-effort frame is interrupted when the time-critical slice begins, so the guard band (G) can shrink. Part 2 follows after the slice and is reassembled in the next switch."
/>

[The TAS post](/blog/time-aware-shaper) explains why a schedule needs a guard band as long as the largest interfering frame. With preemption, the guard band only has to cover the largest piece that cannot be cut. 802.1Qbu lets the gate control list hold back preemptable traffic slightly before a protected window opens and release it afterwards. The window then starts on an idle link [2][3].

Preemption reduces delay variation for express traffic and gives preemptable traffic back the bandwidth that a full guard band would waste [3].

## 5. What preemption does not change

The scheduling study of [the TAS post](/blog/time-aware-shaper) compares preemption with a scheduler that fragments large payloads into separate frames [4]. Two observations:

- **Preemption does not shorten store-and-forward delay.** The next bridge reassembles a preempted frame before forwarding it, so the frame still crosses each hop as one unit. Splitting a payload into separate frames, by contrast, lets the first part move on while the rest is still being sent.
- **Preemption can make an infeasible schedule feasible.** Without it, some express streams miss their deadlines behind long frames. Because fragmentation happens only when needed, preemption also wastes less bandwidth than always splitting [4].

For worst-case analysis, preemption changes two terms. The blocking of an express class by lower priorities drops from one maximum frame to one non-preemptable piece. Preemptable traffic pays a small overhead for each interruption, because every fragment carries its own preamble, header and check sequence.

## 6. Summary

| Property | Frame preemption |
| --- | --- |
| Standards | IEEE 802.3br (Ethernet), IEEE 802.1Qbu (bridge) |
| Acts on | The MAC of one link |
| Per-priority setting | Express or preemptable |
| Minimum fragment | 64 bytes |
| Needs time synchronisation | No (only when combined with scheduled traffic) |
| Gain | Blocking by lower priorities drops from one frame to about a hundred bytes |
