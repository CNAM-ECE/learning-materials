[Time-Sensitive Networking](https://1.ieee802.org/tsn/) (TSN) is a set of [IEEE 802.1](https://1.ieee802.org/) standards for time-sensitive transmission over [Ethernet](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/802.3/10422/). This post goes through the tools one by one, always asking the same question: which part of an ordinary Ethernet [bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_bridge) does this tool change?

## 1. Definition

**TSN** is the set of standards produced by the TSN task group of IEEE 802.1 to provide deterministic services over Ethernet ([IEEE 802](https://www.ieee802.org/)) networks. Most of them are extensions of Bridges and Bridged Networks ([IEEE 802.1Q](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/802.1Q/10323/)), the standard for bridges and [virtual LANs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VLAN), and aim at very low [latency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latency_(engineering)) and high availability [1][2]. The organisation behind them is described in [the IEEE 802.1 post](/blog/ieee-802-1-tsn-task-group).

Figure 1 is the picture to keep in mind. A bridge already has a control plane and a data plane made of ingress processing, a relay, queues, transmission selection and a MAC [3]. Every TSN tool extends one of those existing stages. This is why TSN traffic and ordinary traffic can share a bridge: the eight priorities of 802.1Q are kept for backward compatibility, and the tools are added around them [2].

The bridge itself (its control and data planes, the actions of each port and the steps of the forwarding process) is described in [the bridge post](/blog/ieee-802-1q-bridge).

### The TSN standards at a glance

TSN is not one standard but a family. Each standard covers one function, and a system designer combines the ones a system needs, which is how TSN lets IT and OT traffic share one Ethernet connection [5]. The table follows the overview of the main TSN standards in [5], with each name linked to its post in this series and two standards added that complete the set.

| Standard | Name | What it adds | Group |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [802.1AS-2020](/blog/ieee-802-1as-time-synchronization) | Timing and Synchronization | A common time in every bridge and end station (gPTP) | Time synchronisation |
| [802.1Qav](/blog/credit-based-shaper) | Forwarding and Queuing Enhancements for time-sensitive streams | The credit-based shaper, which spreads reserved traffic over time | Bounded latency |
| [802.1Qbv](/blog/time-aware-shaper) | Enhancements for Scheduled Traffic | Gates opened and closed on a timetable (time-aware shaper) | Bounded latency |
| [802.1Qbu](/blog/ieee-802-1qbu-frame-preemption) | Frame Preemption | Express frames interrupt preemptable ones (with [802.3br](/blog/ieee-802-3br-interspersing-express-traffic) in the Ethernet MAC) | Bounded latency |
| [802.1Qch](/blog/cyclic-queuing-and-forwarding) | Cyclic Queuing and Forwarding | Two queues per class alternate, one hop per cycle | Bounded latency |
| [802.1Qcr](/blog/asynchronous-traffic-shaping) | Asynchronous Traffic Shaping | Per-stream token-bucket shaping without a clock (not in [5]) | Bounded latency |
| [802.1Qci](/blog/per-stream-filtering-and-policing) | Per-Stream Filtering and Policing | Each stream checked against its contract at ingress | Reliability |
| [802.1CB](/blog/frame-replication-and-elimination) | Frame Replication and Elimination for Reliability | Copies over disjoint paths, duplicates removed | Reliability |
| [802.1Qca](/blog/ieee-802-1qca-path-control) | Path Control and Reservation | Explicit, non-shortest paths installed through IS-IS | Reliability |
| [802.1Qat](/blog/ieee-802-1qat-srp) | Stream Reservation Protocol | Distributed admission control, hop by hop | Resources and configuration |
| [802.1Qcc](/blog/sdn-and-tsn-configuration) | Stream Reservation Protocol enhancements and TSN configuration | Centralised configuration with a CUC and a CNC | Resources and configuration |

On top of these base standards, **profiles** select the tools and settings for one application: [802.1BA](/blog/ieee-802-1ba-avb-profile) for audio and video, [802.1CM](/blog/ieee-802-1cm-fronthaul-profile) for mobile fronthaul, [IEC/IEEE 60802](/blog/iec-ieee-60802-industrial-profile) for industrial automation and [802.1DG](/blog/ieee-802-1dg-automotive-profile) for vehicles. The rest of this post goes through the four groups in turn.

### Why standard Ethernet is not enough

Ordinary network equipment has no notion of time, and nothing at the link layer prevents [congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_congestion). Even with eight strict priorities, a frame of the highest priority can wait behind a lower-priority frame whose transmission has already started [2]. TSN addresses both problems.

### Basic terms

From the point of view of 802.1 there are two kinds of device, bridges and end stations [3]:

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Talker | The end station that is the source of a stream |
| Listener | An end station that receives a stream |
| Stream | A unidirectional flow of data from one talker to one or more listeners |
| Bridge | The relay between LANs; its data plane has a MAC relay and at least two ports |

Three kinds of traffic then coexist on the network: time-triggered traffic, rate-constrained streams and [best-effort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best-effort_delivery) traffic [2].

## 2. Four groups of tools

<SourceFigure
  id="farkas2018-components"
  caption="Figure 2. The TSN toolbox: four groups of standards and projects, which together give zero congestion loss and bounded latency. Projects whose name starts with “P” were still in progress in 2018."
/>

The task group presents its standards as a toolbox with four groups: time synchronisation, bounded low latency, ultra reliability, and dedicated resources with their management interfaces [3][4]. A tool can be used on its own, but the full guarantee comes from combining them [2]. The sections below follow this grouping.

## 3. Time synchronisation: 802.1AS

*In depth: [the 802.1AS post](/blog/ieee-802-1as-time-synchronization).*

[IEEE 802.1AS](https://1.ieee802.org/tsn/802-1as-2020/) defines the generalised [Precision Time Protocol](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/1588/6825/) (gPTP), a tightly constrained profile of [IEEE 1588](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/1588/6825/) that distributes time through the network itself with sub-microsecond precision [2]. The 2020 revision improved the accuracy of time measurement and added multiple time domains for redundancy [2]; work on the revision also covered [link aggregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_aggregation), faster change-over between grandmaster clocks, and redundant paths and grandmasters [4].

In Figure 1, time is the bar under the whole data plane. Gates, cyclic queuing and time-gated [policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_policing_(communications)) are meaningless without it, whereas the credit-based shaper and asynchronous shaping do not need it.

## 4. Bounded low latency

### Credit-Based Shaper: 802.1Qav

*In depth: [the credit-based shaper post](/blog/credit-based-shaper).*

The oldest shaper, inherited from [AVB](https://www.ieee802.org/1/pages/avbridges.html), spreads the frames of a class evenly over time so that bursts do not overflow buffers downstream. Each shaped queue has a credit, counted in bits, and may transmit only while its credit is not negative. The slope at which credit accumulates is the bandwidth reserved for the class [2].

AVB defines two shaped classes. Class A has a 125 µs transmission period and a target worst-case latency of 2 ms over seven hops; class B has a 250 µs period and a 50 ms target. Together they may use at most 75 % of the link bandwidth [2]. Combined with stream reservation, the shaper gives a delay below 250 µs per bridge [4], which is too large for many control applications [2] and motivated the tools that follow.

### Scheduled traffic: 802.1Qbv

<ScheduledTrafficFigure />

*In depth: [the Time-Aware Shaper post](/blog/time-aware-shaper).*

A transmission gate is associated with each queue. A queue whose gate is closed is not considered by transmission selection; a queue whose gate is open is served by its usual algorithm. Gates follow a list that repeats periodically, relative to the common time base [3][4]. The result divides time on the link into windows reserved for given priorities, in the manner of [time-division multiple access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-division_multiple_access) [2].

This mechanism is the Time-Aware Shaper of the analysis literature. The service it offers to a queue is derived in [the service curves post](/blog/service-curves).

### Frame preemption: 802.3br and 802.1Qbu

<PreemptionFigure />

*In depth: [the 802.3br post](/blog/ieee-802-3br-interspersing-express-traffic) for the Ethernet side and [the 802.1Qbu post](/blog/ieee-802-1qbu-frame-preemption) for the bridge side.*

A gate does not help if a long frame is already on the wire when the critical window opens. Preemption lets an *express* frame suspend the transmission of a *preemptable* frame, which resumes afterwards. Each traffic-class queue is assigned one of the two statuses [4]. The MAC is split into an express MAC and a preemptable MAC above a merge sublayer; the minimum fragment is 64 bytes including its check sequence [4].

Preemption reduces [delay variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packet_delay_variation) for express traffic and returns to preemptable traffic the bandwidth that a guard band would otherwise waste. It is local to a link, not a form of [IP](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc791) fragmentation [3].

### Cyclic Queuing and Forwarding: 802.1Qch

<CyclicQueuingFigure />

*In depth: [the CQF post](/blog/cyclic-queuing-and-forwarding).*

Two buffers per class are filled and drained in alternate cycles, synchronised across the network. Frames received during one cycle are sent during the next, so a frame stays one cycle at each hop [4]. Latency then depends only on the cycle time and the hop count, not on the topology or on the other flows [2].

### Asynchronous Traffic Shaping: 802.1Qcr

*In depth: [the ATS post](/blog/asynchronous-traffic-shaping).*

Asynchronous Traffic Shaping ([ATS](https://1.ieee802.org/tsn/802-1qcr/)) achieves zero congestion loss without time synchronisation. Traffic is re-shaped at every hop, and urgent traffic is prioritised over relaxed traffic. It resembles per-flow shaping, with one important difference: all streams going from one input port to one output port share a queue, and the appropriate shaper state is applied to each frame in front of that queue [3]. The shaper applies a [token-bucket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Token_bucket) rule using local clocks and assigns each frame an *eligibility time*; strict-priority selection then operates on eligible frames [3].

ATS reuses the per-stream stages shown in the next figure. Its [network calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_calculus) model, the interleaved regulator, is discussed in [the TSN shapers post](/blog/network-calculus-for-tsn).

## 5. Ultra reliability

### Per-Stream Filtering and Policing: 802.1Qci

<PerStreamFigure />

*In depth: [the PSFP post](/blog/per-stream-filtering-and-policing).*

Guarantees computed for well-behaved streams are worthless if one talker exceeds its contract. [PSFP](https://1.ieee802.org/tsn/802-1qci/) protects the network against bandwidth violation, malfunction and attack by taking decisions per stream at the ingress [3]. A frame passes through three elements [3][4]:

1. a **stream filter**, which identifies the stream and selects the gate and meter to apply;
2. a **stream gate**, open or closed on a time schedule, which can also set the internal priority of the frame;
3. a **flow meter**, which enforces a bandwidth profile and marks frames red, yellow or green.

In the terms of this series, PSFP is what makes the declared [arrival curve](/blog/arrival-curves) an enforced one.

*In depth: [the FRER post](/blog/frame-replication-and-elimination).*

[FRER](https://1.ieee802.org/tsn/802-1cb/) avoids frame loss caused by equipment failure. It is a per-frame 1+1 (or 1+n) redundancy: frames are numbered, sent on two or more maximally disjoint paths, then recombined with the extra copies deleted. There is no failure detection and no switch-over, hence no interruption when a path fails [3].

### Path control: 802.1Qca

Disjoint paths have to be installed. [802.1Qca](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/802.1Qca/5557/) extends the [IS-IS](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1195) routing protocol beyond shortest-path trees with *explicit trees*, in a hybrid arrangement: IS-IS provides topology discovery and default paths, while one or more controllers define the explicit trees [3].

## 6. Dedicated resources and configuration

### Stream reservation: 802.1Qat

*In depth: [the stream reservation post](/blog/stream-reservation).*

The Stream Reservation Protocol performs distributed [admission control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admission_control). A talker advertises a stream; the advertisement propagates towards the listeners while the worst-case latency is accumulated; a listener that accepts triggers the reservation of bandwidth back along the path. Streams are identified by a 64-bit stream identifier, made of a 48-bit [MAC address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAC_address) and a 16-bit unique identifier [2].

### TSN configuration: 802.1Qcc

<SourceFigure
  id="farkas2018-configuration"
  caption="Figure 8. The three configuration models of 802.1Qcc: fully distributed, centralized network and distributed user, and fully centralized."
/>

*In depth: [the SDN post](/blog/sdn-and-tsn-configuration).*

[802.1Qcc](https://1.ieee802.org/tsn/802-1qcc/) enhances the reservation protocol and defines three configuration models [3]:

- **fully distributed**: end stations and bridges exchange requirements hop by hop;
- **centralised network, distributed user**: end stations still signal to the network edge, but a central entity computes and configures;
- **fully centralised**: a Centralized User Configuration entity collects the requirements of the end stations and passes them to a Centralized Network Configuration entity.

The CNC has a view of the whole network, performs reservation and scheduling centrally, and configures bridges through [NETCONF](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6241) or [RESTCONF](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8040) with [YANG](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7950) data models [2]. This is the architecture assumed by most work on online reconfiguration, including [the reconfiguration post](/blog/dynamic-reconfiguration-of-tsn): the CNC is the place where an admission test must run.
